# Technical Concept: Pearls EcoFuel Solutions

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Proposed concept; technical validation pending  
**Organization:** Pearls for Change Foundation  
**Proposed area:** Kisumu County, Kenya

## 1. Purpose
Assess the feasibility of converting suitable locally available biomass residues into bio-oil, non-condensable gas, and char through a professionally designed and controlled pyrolysis process.

## 2. Problem statement
Some agricultural and other organic residues may be underutilized or poorly managed, while communities need sustainable livelihood opportunities and appropriate energy options. The project will investigate whether a responsibly sourced biomass-to-products pathway is technically, environmentally, socially, and economically appropriate for the local context.

The project will not assume that all residues are waste: some materials have existing uses as animal feed, soil amendments, household fuel, or industrial inputs. Those uses and the consequences of removing materials must be assessed first.

## 3. Conceptual process
At a high level, pyrolysis is the thermal decomposition of organic material under oxygen-limited conditions. A suitably engineered system may produce:
- Condensable vapours collected as a liquid bio-oil.
- Non-condensable gases.
- A solid char fraction.

This document intentionally does not specify construction details, operating settings, or instructions for running equipment. Reactor design, process control, gas management, emissions control, and emergency systems must be assessed by qualified professionals.

## 4. Candidate feedstocks
The following are candidates for assessment, not confirmed inputs:
- Rice husks.
- Maize cobs.
- Sugarcane bagasse.
- Water hyacinth.
- Coffee husks.
- Other locally available agricultural residues.

For each candidate, a feasibility study should record seasonal availability, collection distance, moisture and ash characteristics, contamination risks, existing uses, land and ecosystem impacts, storage needs, and supply consistency. Water hyacinth collection must consider ecological impacts and any relevant local permissions.

## 5. Product streams and validation needs

### Bio-oil
A complex liquid mixture whose water content, acidity, stability, solids, energy content, and composition can vary. It must be characterized and safely stored and handled. It should not be described as diesel or a drop-in transport fuel unless appropriate upgrading, standards compliance, and independent testing demonstrate that claim.

### Non-condensable gas
May contain combustible and hazardous components. Any collection, use, treatment, or flaring strategy requires engineered safeguards, monitoring, and professional assessment. It must not be vented or used casually.

### Char
A carbon-rich solid, not automatically suitable for soil use, cooking, or sale. Intended use should be determined only after appropriate testing for relevant physical and chemical properties and possible contaminants.

## 6. Technical questions to resolve
1. Which feedstock is most consistently available and has the lowest environmental and social trade-offs?
2. Which process configuration best matches the desired primary product and local conditions?
3. What pre-treatment and quality controls would be needed?
4. What are the expected material and energy balances, based on credible studies or measured trials?
5. How will emissions, wastewater or condensates, residues, fire risk, and worker exposure be managed?
6. What testing is needed for each product and its intended application?
7. What are the capital, operating, maintenance, logistics, and compliance costs?
8. What permissions, environmental assessments, standards, and licences may apply in Kenya?

## 7. Environmental, health and safety considerations
Before any trial, qualified partners should complete a risk assessment and identify applicable approvals. The plan should address emissions and air quality, fire and thermal hazards, pressure and gas hazards, exposure to vapours, safe material storage, contaminated feedstock, spill response, waste disposal, monitoring, training, and emergency procedures.

Potential environmental benefits must be assessed across the whole supply chain, including collection, transport, process energy, emissions, product use, and final disposal. Do not claim net greenhouse-gas reductions without a defensible assessment.

## 8. Social inclusion
The project intends to explore fair participation and livelihood opportunities for women and young people. Future engagement should be voluntary, accessible, transparent, and informed by community consultation. Participation targets should be set after the project scope and resources are confirmed.

## 9. Evidence required before claims of success
- Feedstock characterization and supply evidence.
- Engineering review and documented process design.
- Required approvals and risk assessments.
- Independently or appropriately verified product tests.
- Emissions and environmental monitoring data.
- Material and energy balance with methods explained.
- Cost and market assessment.
- Community feedback and documented outcomes.

## 10. Limitations
No prototype, production trial, product yield, product quality, emissions performance, cost-effectiveness, or commercial outcome is claimed as completed in this version.
